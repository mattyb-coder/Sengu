water resource authority managment

A Water Resource Management Authority is a statutory body responsible for regulating, managing, developing, protecting, and conserving a country’s water resources—examples include Jamaica’s Water Resources Authority (WRA) and Zambia’s Water Resources Management Authority (WARMA). Overview Several countries operate agencies titled Water Resource Management Authority or similar, each mandated to oversee sustainable water use, allocation, and protection. 1. Jamaica – Water Resources Authority (WRA) The Water Resources Authority in Jamaica manages, protects, and allocates the nation’s water resources. It maintains hydrological databases, licenses water abstraction, monitors streamflow at 133 stations, and groundwater at 320 wells. It also participates in environmental impact assessments and national water‑quality standards. 2. Zambia – Water Resources Management Authority (WARMA) WARMA is established under the Water Resources Management Act No. 21 of 2011. It regulates, manages, develops, protects, and conserves Zambia’s water resources. WARMA operates through catchment offices, promotes integrated water‑resource management, sets standards, identifies freshwater sources, and publishes water‑resource forecasts. 3. Related U.S. Example – Bureau of Reclamation While not named “Water Resource Management Authority,” the U.S. Bureau of Reclamation performs similar functions in managing water and power resources, drought response, and major water‑supply projects across the western United States.
